Dealing with Red Wine Stains on Wool Sweaters

Blot red wine on a wool sweater right away—don’t rub. Use cool water and a small amount of gentle wool-safe detergent to lift the stain, then rinse and air-dry flat.

If the stain is set or the sweater is labeled dry-clean only, stop after blotting and take it to a professional cleaner.

Steps to Treat Red Wine on a Wool Sweater

  1. Act fast and blot. Lay the sweater flat and blot the stain with a clean, dry cloth or paper towel to absorb as much wine as possible.
  2. Support the fabric. Put a clean towel underneath the stained area so the wine doesn’t spread through other layers.
  3. Rinse with cool water from the back. Run a small stream of cool water through the stain from the inside of the sweater outward to push wine out of the fibers.
  4. Apply a tiny amount of wool-safe detergent. Work a drop into the stain with your fingertips using a light dabbing motion (no scrubbing). Let it sit briefly.
  5. Rinse thoroughly. Rinse with cool water until the water runs clear and no suds remain, which helps prevent ring marks.
  6. Blot and reshape. Press (don’t wring) between clean towels to remove water, then gently reshape the sweater.
  7. Air-dry flat. Dry flat on a towel away from heat and sun; check the stain before the sweater is fully dry.
  8. Repeat if needed or escalate. If color remains, repeat once. If it persists or the label says dry-clean only, take it to a cleaner and mention it was red wine.

What Not to Do

  • Don’t rub or scrub. This can felt wool and spread the stain.
  • Don’t use hot water. Heat can set the stain and shrink wool.
  • Don’t wring or twist. It can distort the sweater’s shape.
  • Don’t use bleach or harsh stain removers. They can damage wool fibers and dye.
  • Don’t put it in a dryer. Heat and tumbling can shrink or felt the sweater and lock in remaining stain.

Notes for Common Situations

Stain treatment urgency: The sooner you blot and rinse, the better. If you can’t treat immediately, keep blotting and use cool water as soon as possible; avoid letting the area dry before any rinse.

Wool fabric characteristics: Wool is sensitive to heat, agitation, and strong chemicals. Gentle dabbing, cool water, and a wool-safe detergent help avoid felting, fading, and texture changes.

Previous methods attempted: If you already rubbed the stain, used heat, or applied a strong product, stop and rinse out any residue with cool water. If the stain remains or the fabric texture has changed, professional dry cleaning is the safest next step.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
