How to Remove Ink Stains from Polyester

Dab rubbing alcohol onto the ink stain — polyester and ballpoint ink are both petrochemical-based, which makes them bond strongly but also means the same solvent dissolves both the ink resin and the oil carrier [S1]. Place paper towels behind the fabric, apply isopropanol with a cotton swab, and blot with a clean white cloth. Repeat until no ink transfers, then soak in Sil Fleckensalz (Grade 2.4) for 30 minutes to oxidize remaining dye. Act before any heat exposure.

How to Remove Ink from Polyester — Step by Step

  1. Place paper towels behind the stain. Layer several paper towels or a white cloth behind the stained area. Dissolved ink will migrate through the fabric — you need to capture it.
  2. Test alcohol on a hidden area. Apply a small drop of isopropanol to an inside seam. Wait 2 minutes. If no color change occurs, proceed. (This is extra important on printed polyester.)
  3. Apply rubbing alcohol to the stain. Use a cotton swab or eyedropper to apply isopropanol directly to the ink mark. The alcohol dissolves the ink's resin binder and oil carrier, releasing the dye from the polyester surface [S1]. Don't saturate — use controlled amounts to prevent spreading.
  4. Blot with a clean white cloth. Press firmly to transfer dissolved ink. Rotate to a clean section of the cloth after each blot. Repeat the alcohol-and-blot cycle until no more color transfers — this may take 5–15 rounds for heavy stains.
  5. Rinse with warm water. Flush to remove alcohol and dissolved ink residue. Warm water (not hot) works best for polyester.
  6. Soak in oxygen bleach. Dissolve 1–2 tablespoons Sil 1 für Alles Fleckensalz in warm water (40°C). Soak for 30–60 minutes. Sil's active oxygen targets any remaining ink dye through oxidative chromophore destruction [S2].
  7. Machine wash at 40°C. Wash at the standard polyester temperature. Inspect before drying — never apply heat until the ink is completely gone.

What Not to Do

  • Don't use the dryer while ink remains. Polyester is a thermoplastic. Heat fuses ink resin into the fiber structure, making removal nearly impossible [S3].
  • Don't use acetone on polyester. While effective on ink, acetone dissolves polyester fiber itself. Stick to isopropanol, which is safe for polyester.
  • Don't rub the stain. Rubbing spreads ink across the polyester surface and pushes it deeper into the fiber structure.
  • Don't use bleach on colored polyester. Chlorine bleach can permanently damage polyester dyes and weaken fibers. Oxygen bleach (Sil) is color-safe.

Why Ink on Polyester Is a Special Challenge

Polyester and ballpoint ink share a molecular kinship — both are petrochemical products [S1]. Polyester (polyethylene terephthalate) and the oil-resin carrier in ink are both derived from similar hydrocarbon chemistry. This creates a strong molecular affinity through van der Waals forces, meaning ink doesn't just sit on polyester — it bonds. The resin in ballpoint ink is particularly problematic because it hardens after application, creating a physical lock on the fiber surface. Rubbing alcohol works by re-dissolving this resin, while Sil's oxidative bleaching destroys the freed dye molecules.

Best for Synthetic Ink Stains

Sil 1 für Alles Fleckensalz

Grade 2.4

Why Sil works for ink on polyester: Sil serves as the essential second step after alcohol pre-treatment. While alcohol dissolves the resin carrier, ink dye molecules remain bonded to polyester through disperse dye interactions — the same mechanism used to intentionally dye polyester in manufacturing [S2]. Sil's sodium percarbonate generates active oxygen that performs oxidative chromophore cleavage, breaking the dye's color-producing molecular bonds into colorless fragments. This is the only reliable way to remove ink dye that has dispersed into polyester fiber.

For ink on polyester: Always pre-treat with rubbing alcohol first. Then soak in Sil at 40°C for 30–60 minutes. Don't exceed 60°C — polyester deforms at high temperatures. For stubborn stains, extend soaking to 4 hours.
