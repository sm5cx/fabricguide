What to Do Immediately After a Red Wine Spill on Upholstery

Blot the red wine immediately with clean, dry cloths, working from the outside of the stain toward the center, and switch to fresh cloth sections often. Then lightly dab with a small amount of cool water to dilute and keep blotting until you stop lifting color.

Immediate steps to take

  1. Act fast and protect the area. Remove any items on the cushion and, if possible, lift the cushion off the furniture. Slide a towel or paper towels under/behind the fabric area if wine could soak through.
  2. Blot—don’t rub. Using a clean, white, absorbent cloth or paper towels, blot straight down. Work from the outer edge toward the center to avoid spreading.
  3. Keep switching to clean absorbent material. As soon as the cloth looks wet or tinted, rotate to a dry spot or use a new cloth so you keep pulling wine up rather than reapplying it.
  4. Dilute with cool water in small amounts. Lightly dampen a cloth with cool water and dab the stain (don’t pour). Follow immediately with dry blotting. Repeat in short cycles.
  5. If the cushion cover is removable, check the care label. If it’s washable, remove it promptly and rinse the stained area from the back with cool water (if feasible), then launder per the label. If it’s not washable or not removable, continue careful blot/dab cycles.
  6. Use a mild cleaning solution only after you’ve lifted as much wine as possible. If you choose to try a gentle DIY mix (for example, a small amount of mild dish soap in cool water), test on an inconspicuous area first. Dab sparingly, then blot with plain cool water to remove residue.
  7. Dry the area thoroughly. Press with dry towels to remove moisture. Let it air-dry; if you can, keep airflow moving across the cushion and avoid using heat directly on the spot.
  8. Reassess once it’s nearly dry. Stains can look lighter when wet and reappear as they dry. If color remains after blotting and gentle cleaning, pause DIY efforts and consider professional upholstery cleaning.

What not to do

  • Don’t rub or scrub. This can push wine deeper and damage upholstery fibers.
  • Don’t use hot water or heat to dry. Heat can set a red wine stain and make it harder to remove.
  • Don’t over-wet the cushion. Soaking can drive the stain into padding and slow drying, increasing odor or watermark risk.
  • Don’t use colored towels. Dyes can transfer onto damp upholstery.
  • Don’t mix multiple cleaners. Combining products can create residue or damage fabric, and makes results unpredictable.
  • Don’t keep escalating if the fabric reacts poorly. If you see color transfer, fuzzing, or distortion, stop and switch to a safer approach (or a pro).

Notes for common situations

The stain has already soaked in: Focus on repeated blot/dilute cycles with minimal moisture, then let it dry and reassess. If the cushion feels wet through or you suspect the padding is affected, faster drying and professional help can prevent lingering discoloration.

You need to respond quickly to prevent further damage: Your biggest wins are speed and restraint—blot immediately, use cool water sparingly, and avoid rubbing. Even 2–3 minutes of blotting can remove a surprising amount before it sets.

You may need to assess if DIY treatment is effective: If color stops lifting onto your cloth, the stain remains obvious after partial drying, or the upholstery shows water rings or texture change, stop experimenting and consider a professional upholstery cleaner.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
