How to Stop Red Wine Stains on Your Carpet

Blot up as much red wine as possible right away, then rinse the spot with a small amount of cold water and blot again. Follow with a gentle cleaning solution and keep blotting from the outside in to stop the stain from spreading and setting.

Immediate steps to treat red wine on carpet

  1. Blot immediately. Use clean paper towels or a white cloth to blot (don’t rub). Press firmly to lift liquid from the carpet fibers.
  2. Work from the outside in. Keep blotting toward the center of the spill to avoid spreading the stain into clean carpet.
  3. Add cold water sparingly. Dampen the stained area with a small amount of cold water, then blot again to dilute and lift more wine.
  4. Apply a mild cleaner. Use a small amount of gentle dish soap mixed with water (or another mild carpet-safe cleaner). Lightly dab it onto the stain, then blot.
  5. Repeat in short cycles. Alternate between light dabs of solution and blotting with a clean section of cloth until transfer slows.
  6. Rinse the area. Dampen with plain cold water to remove soap/cleaner residue, then blot thoroughly.
  7. Dry the spot. Press dry towels over the area until it feels only slightly damp. If possible, place a dry towel over it and weigh it down briefly to wick remaining moisture.
  8. Let it air-dry fully. Once dry, gently lift fibers with your fingers if they feel matted, and check if any pink tint remains to decide if another pass is needed.

What not to do

  • Don’t rub or scrub. This can push wine deeper into the fibers and enlarge the stain.
  • Don’t use hot water. Heat can help set a dye-based stain.
  • Don’t over-soak the carpet. Too much liquid can spread the stain and soak the padding underneath.
  • Don’t use colored towels. Dyes can transfer onto light carpet; stick to white cloths/paper towels.
  • Don’t mix multiple strong cleaners. Combining products can damage fibers or create unwanted fumes.
  • Don’t stop while it’s still wet and red. Keep blotting and diluting until little to no color transfers to your towel.

Notes for common situations

Act quickly to prevent staining: The sooner you blot and dilute, the less time the wine has to bind to carpet fibers. Even a minute or two can make a difference, so start with dry blotting before you look for products.

Assess the extent of the spill: If the spill is large or has reached the padding, focus on removing as much liquid as possible without flooding the area. Persistent odor, recurring dampness, or a stain that reappears after drying can be a sign the underlayer needs deeper cleaning.

Gather necessary cleaning materials: A stack of white towels/paper towels, a bowl of cold water, and a small amount of mild dish soap are usually enough for immediate treatment. If you have a carpet-safe cleaner, test it on an inconspicuous spot first and follow the label directions.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
