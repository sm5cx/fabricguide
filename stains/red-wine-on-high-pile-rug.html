Managing Red Wine Stains on High-Pile Rugs

Blot red wine on a high-pile rug immediately with clean, dry towels to pull liquid up from the fibers—don’t rub. Then work a small amount of cool water into the spot and keep blotting until transfer slows; stop before the rug feels soaked.

Steps to take right away

  1. Act fast and clear the area. Remove glasses/bottles and keep foot traffic off the spot so the wine isn’t pushed deeper into the pile.
  2. Lift up liquid from the top of the pile. Use white paper towels or a clean white cloth and press straight down. Replace towels as they turn pink/red.
  3. Get into the pile without rubbing. Gently part the fibers with your fingers and continue pressing so towels contact the damp base of the pile.
  4. Add a small amount of cool water. Lightly dampen a fresh cloth (or apply a tiny splash to the cloth, not the rug) and press again to dilute. Repeat: dampen, press, replace cloth.
  5. Work from the outside in. Blot the outer edge first, then move toward the center to reduce spreading and rings.
  6. Control moisture. High-pile rugs hold water; use minimal liquid each round and keep blotting until the spot feels only slightly damp.
  7. Dry the area thoroughly. Stack dry towels and weigh them down for 10–20 minutes, then swap once more if needed to pull remaining moisture up.
  8. Let it air out. Leave the rug uncovered and avoid walking on it until it feels dry to the touch; gently fluff the pile with your fingers after drying.

What not to do

  • Don’t rub or scrub. This drives wine deeper and can fuzz or distort high-pile fibers.
  • Don’t pour lots of water on the rug. Over-wetting can push stain into the backing/pad and make drying difficult.
  • Don’t use hot water or heat to “set” dry it. Heat can make discoloration harder to remove and may affect some fibers.
  • Don’t use colored towels or dyed cloths. Dye can transfer onto damp fibers.
  • Don’t keep working once no more color lifts. Overworking can spread the stain and create a larger damp area.
  • Don’t mix multiple cleaners at once. If you choose to use a cleaner later, test first and use one method at a time.

Notes for your situation

Assess the extent of the stain: If the spill is large, has soaked through to the backing, or covers a wide area, prioritize blotting and drying to prevent wicking. A big, deep spill may warrant professional cleaning to fully rinse and dry the pile and backing.

Determine if the rug is absorbent: High-pile rugs often absorb and hold liquid below the surface. If the surface looks better but towels still pick up color when you press into the pile, keep using small amounts of cool water and blotting cycles rather than adding more liquid at once.

Check for existing stains or damage: Pre-existing spots, worn areas, or damaged backing can make the rug more prone to rings and texture changes. In those areas, use extra-light moisture and gentler pressure, and stop early if fibers start matting or shedding.

If you’re unsure about the rug’s fiber type or it’s a valuable/antique piece, minimize moisture, keep blotting, and consider contacting a rug cleaner before trying stronger treatments.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
