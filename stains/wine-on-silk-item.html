Should I Photograph a Wine Stain Before Dry Cleaning a Silk Item?

Yes—take clear photos of the wine stain before you send a silk item to the dry cleaner. Quick documentation preserves what the stain looked like, can help you and the cleaner discuss treatment, and gives you a record if questions come up later.

How to photograph the stain (quick and useful)

  1. Photograph it before any cleaning. Take photos as-is, before blotting, rinsing, steaming, or applying any product.
  2. Use bright, even light. Stand near a window or under a strong lamp; avoid harsh flash if it creates glare that hides the stain’s edges.
  3. Get a wide shot for context. Capture the whole item so the cleaner can see where the stain sits (hem, collar, seam, lining area).
  4. Take close-ups from multiple angles. Shoot straight on and slightly angled to show texture and any “tide lines” where the stain ends.
  5. Include a simple size reference. Place a common object (like a coin) near the stain without covering it, so the stain’s size is clear.
  6. Capture color accurately. Take a couple of photos with and without flash (if available) and choose the ones that best match what you see in person.
  7. Document any spread or changes. If the stain looks larger or lighter/darker over time, take an updated photo before drop-off.
  8. Save and share the photos. Keep originals (don’t over-edit) and show them at intake so the cleaner knows what to target and what “before” looked like.

What not to do

  • Don’t delay cleaning just to get perfect photos. Take them quickly and proceed with professional care.
  • Don’t rub or wet the silk to “improve” the picture. Extra handling can spread the wine or set a ring.
  • Don’t use heavy filters or aggressive editing. It can distort color and make documentation less credible.
  • Don’t photograph in low light or with strong glare. Blurry or reflective images often hide the stain’s true boundary.
  • Don’t omit where the stain is located. Only close-ups without a context shot can be hard to interpret later.

Notes for common situations

Consider the risk of further damage during cleaning. Silk is delicate, and some stains can leave a mark even after professional treatment. Photos help set expectations about what was present before cleaning and can support a clearer conversation about possible outcomes.

Note if the stain has spread since the incident. Wine can wick through fabric or into seams and layers. If you see spreading, photograph the edges and any lighter “halo” so changes are documented before the item is handled further.

Document for insurance or dispute purposes. If the silk item is valuable or the cleaning is being reimbursed, keep dated photos showing the stain’s condition prior to drop-off. When possible, also photograph any existing wear (snags, pulls, fading) to avoid confusion about what happened when.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
