Managing a Red Wine Stain on Your Armchair Cushion

Press (blot) a red wine spill on an armchair cushion—don’t leave it alone. Use a clean, dry cloth to lift as much liquid as possible without rubbing, then lightly damp-blot to dilute the stain.

What to Do Now

  1. Act quickly and set up blotting. Put a clean white cloth or paper towels on the stain and apply firm, even pressure to absorb wine.
  2. Keep blotting with fresh cloth. Lift and replace to a dry area often; stop when little color transfers.
  3. Dilute with small amounts of cool water. Lightly dampen a cloth (don’t pour directly onto the cushion) and blot from the outside edge toward the center to control spread.
  4. Repeat dry-blot then damp-blot cycles. The goal is to lift and dilute, not to scrub. Continue until transfer is faint.
  5. Use a tiny amount of mild soap only if needed. If color remains, add a drop of mild liquid soap to cool water, dampen a cloth, and blot gently; then blot again with plain water to remove soap residue.
  6. Remove excess moisture. Press with dry towels to pull out as much water as possible from the cushion surface.
  7. Air-dry thoroughly. Let the cushion dry with good airflow; rotate or stand it so moisture can escape. Fluff/reshape when nearly dry.
  8. Recheck when dry. If a shadow remains, repeat gentle blotting rather than escalating force; if it worsens or spreads, consider professional cleaning.

What Not to Do

  • Don’t rub or scrub. It can push wine deeper into the upholstery and enlarge the stain.
  • Don’t “leave it to dry.” Set stains are harder to remove than fresh spills.
  • Don’t over-wet the cushion. Soaking can drive pigment into padding and prolong drying.
  • Don’t use hot water or heat to dry. Heat can help set dye-based stains.
  • Don’t use colored towels. Dye transfer can create a second stain.
  • Don’t mix strong cleaners. Avoid untested or harsh chemicals that can discolor or damage upholstery.

Notes for Your Situation

Stain severity and extent: If the spill is large or has reached seams/piping, focus on quick, repeated pressing to remove as much liquid as possible before any damp-blotting. Work in small sections so the stain doesn’t spread.

Time elapsed since spill: The longer it sits, the more likely pigment binds to fibers. If it has already dried, start with gentle cool-water damp-blotting to rehydrate the area, then press dry; you may need a few rounds and more drying time.

Type of upholstery material: Always test any moisture or soap on a hidden spot first. Delicate or textured fabrics can fuzz or distort with friction, so rely on pressing rather than wiping; if the fabric seems to change color/texture when damp, stop and seek professional help.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
