How to Remove Ink Stains from Cotton

Dab rubbing alcohol (isopropanol) directly onto the ink stain with a cotton swab — alcohol dissolves the resin binder that holds ballpoint ink to cotton fibers [S1]. Place paper towels underneath to catch dissolved ink, blot with a clean white cloth, and repeat until no more ink transfers. Then soak in Sil Fleckensalz (Grade 2.4) for 30–60 minutes to lift remaining dye. Never rub — ink spreads rapidly through cotton's absorbent weave.

How to Remove Ink from Cotton — Step by Step

  1. Place paper towels underneath. Put several layers of paper towel (or an old white cloth) behind the stain. As you apply solvent, dissolved ink will pass through the fabric — you want to catch it, not transfer it to the other side of the garment.
  2. Test on a hidden area. If the cotton is colored or printed, test rubbing alcohol on an inside seam first. Alcohol can dissolve some fabric dyes (it won't damage white cotton at all).
  3. Apply rubbing alcohol. Using a cotton swab, eyedropper, or the corner of a clean cloth, dab isopropanol directly onto the ink stain. Don't flood the area — controlled application prevents spreading. The alcohol dissolves the resin binder in ballpoint ink, releasing the dye [S1].
  4. Blot with a clean white cloth. Press firmly to transfer dissolved ink onto the cloth. Move to a fresh section of the cloth with each blot. Repeat alcohol application and blotting 5–10 times until no more ink transfers.
  5. Rinse with cold water. Flush the treated area to remove dissolved ink residue and alcohol.
  6. Soak in oxygen bleach. Dissolve 1–2 tablespoons of Sil 1 für Alles Fleckensalz in warm water (40°C). Soak for 30–60 minutes. Sil's active oxygen targets remaining ink dye molecules through oxidative bleaching — it cleaves the chromophore (color-producing) bonds in the dye [S2].
  7. Machine wash. Wash according to the care label. Inspect before drying — heat from the dryer can set any remaining ink permanently.

What Not to Do

  • Don't rub the stain. Ink pigment spreads rapidly through cotton's absorbent cellulose fibers when rubbed. Always dab and blot.
  • Don't use hot water first. Heat can set certain ink dyes into cotton. Start with cold water and alcohol treatment.
  • Don't use hairspray. The old "hairspray removes ink" trick relied on the high alcohol content of old hairsprays. Modern hairsprays contain less alcohol and more polymers that can actually make the stain worse.
  • Don't skip the paper towel backing. Without absorption underneath, dissolved ink soaks through to the back of the garment, creating a second stain.

Why Ink Stains Require a Solvent Approach

Ballpoint ink is fundamentally different from most stains. It's not water-based (like juice) or fat-based (like grease) — it's a resin-dye suspension in oil [S1]. The ink contains synthetic dyes dissolved in a paste of resins and fatty acids. When it contacts cotton, the oil carrier wicks into the cellulose fibers while the resin hardens, locking the dye in place. Water alone can't dissolve resin, and surfactants can't emulsify it effectively. You need a solvent (alcohol) to re-dissolve the resin and free the dye, then an oxidizing agent (Sil) to destroy the dye molecules themselves.

Best for Ink Dye

Sil 1 für Alles Fleckensalz

Grade 2.4

Why Sil works for ink on cotton: After alcohol dissolves the resin binder, Sil's active oxygen (sodium percarbonate) performs the crucial second step: oxidative dye destruction [S2]. The oxygen radicals attack the chromophore groups in ink dyes — the specific molecular structures that produce color. This breaks the dye into colorless fragments. Standard detergent can wash away loosened ink, but only oxidative bleaching removes dye that has already bonded to cellulose.

For ink stains specifically: Always pre-treat with rubbing alcohol first to dissolve the resin. Then soak in Sil at 40°C for 30–60 minutes. For stubborn ink, soak up to 4 hours. The alcohol + Sil combination tackles both stain components: resin and dye.
