Preventing Sunscreen Stains from Spreading During Washing

To keep sunscreen stains from spreading, stop the wash, keep the stained area dry, and pre-treat the spot before it goes into a full load. Wash the item separately or with similar colors using the coolest effective setting, and check the stain before drying so heat doesn’t set it.

Steps to stop sunscreen stains from spreading

  1. Separate the item right away and avoid rubbing the stained area against other fabric.
  2. If the item is already in the washer, pause the cycle and remove it; don’t let the stain sit in sudsy water with other clothes.
  3. Blot excess product from the fabric with a clean cloth or paper towel (press and lift); do not scrub.
  4. Pre-treat the stained area with a small amount of liquid laundry detergent, working it in gently with your fingers, then let it sit briefly before washing.
  5. Rinse the pre-treated area from the back of the fabric with cool water to push residue out rather than further into the fibers.
  6. Wash the item alone (or with similar colors) on a cool or cold setting using the recommended amount of detergent.
  7. Choose a cycle that matches the fabric (gentle for delicate items, regular for sturdier fabrics) and add an extra rinse if your machine allows it.
  8. After washing, inspect the stain before drying; if you still see discoloration, repeat pre-treatment and rewash rather than using heat.

What not to do

  • Don’t dry the item until you’re sure the stain is gone; heat can make stains harder to remove.
  • Don’t overload the washer, which increases fabric-to-fabric rubbing and can spread residue.
  • Don’t scrub the stain aggressively; it can push product deeper and smear it to clean areas.
  • Don’t wash the stained item with light-colored or delicate pieces if you can avoid it.
  • Don’t use hot water “to fix it” unless the care label allows it and you’ve already tested a cooler wash first.
  • Don’t ignore the care label—settings that are too harsh can damage fabric while the stain remains.

Notes for common situations

Immediate action upon noticing a stain: The sooner you isolate and pre-treat, the less chance the residue has to transfer in the wash. If you can’t wash immediately, keep the stained area from rubbing on other parts of the garment (or other items) and avoid adding water until you can pre-treat.

Type of fabric used in the clothing: Delicate or loosely woven fabrics can be more prone to transfer because product can move across fibers more easily. Use a gentler cycle and minimal agitation for delicate fabrics, and be extra careful not to scrub during pre-treatment.

Wash settings and temperature recommendations: Start with cool or cold water and a normal detergent amount to reduce spreading and avoid setting the stain. If your washer offers it, an extra rinse helps remove leftover product that could redeposit elsewhere.

Before drying: Air-dry or at least re-check under good light; if the stain is still present, repeat pre-treatment and wash again rather than moving to higher heat.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
