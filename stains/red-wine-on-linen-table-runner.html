What to Do Immediately After Red Wine Stains on Linen Table Runners

Blot the red wine immediately with a clean cloth, working from the outside of the stain inward, and keep pressure light so you don’t spread it. Then flush the back of the stain with cold water to push wine out of the linen before it sets. Avoid heat and rubbing until the stain is gone.

Immediate Steps

  1. Act fast. The sooner you start, the less time the wine has to bind to the linen fibers.
  2. Lift the runner and protect the surface. Slide a dry towel or paper towels under the stained area to catch moisture and keep the wine from wicking further.
  3. Blot—don’t rub. Use a clean, dry cloth or paper towel to blot gently. Replace with a fresh area often, and blot from the edges toward the center.
  4. Keep the fabric flat and supported. Don’t bunch or twist the linen; that can spread the stain and drive it deeper.
  5. Flush with cold water from the back. Run cold water through the underside of the stain so it pushes the wine out the way it came in. Continue until the runoff is lighter.
  6. Blot again to remove excess water. Press with a clean towel to absorb moisture without friction.
  7. Repeat the cold-water flush if needed. If you still see strong color, alternate flushing and blotting rather than scrubbing.
  8. Air-dry if you can’t finish right away. Leave it to dry naturally; keep it away from heat sources until you can continue treatment.

What Not to Do

  • Don’t rub or scrub. It frays linen fibers and can spread the stain outward.
  • Don’t use hot water. Heat can help set red wine pigments into fabric.
  • Don’t use a hair dryer or iron. Any heat can make the stain harder to remove later.
  • Don’t wait and “deal with it later” while it’s wet. Letting it sit increases the chance it sets.
  • Don’t over-soak the whole runner. Focus on flushing the stained area to reduce water marks and dye spread.

Notes

Act quickly to prevent the stain from setting. Even a few minutes can make a difference with red wine on linen; start blotting as soon as you notice it.

Blot, don’t rub, to absorb as much wine as possible. Think “press and lift.” Switch to clean, dry material frequently so you’re not reapplying wine to the fabric.

Use cold water before applying any treatments. Cold flushing is a safe first move for linen and helps remove loose pigment before you consider any further stain-removal steps.

If the stain is large or the runner is delicate. Keep handling minimal and prioritize blotting plus cold flushing; avoid aggressive action that can distort the weave.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
