How to Stop Red Wine Stains on Upholstery

Blot immediately with a clean, dry cloth to lift as much wine as possible, then dilute the area with small amounts of cool water and keep blotting. Work in short, gentle cycles to avoid pushing wine deeper into the padding.

Immediate steps for red wine on upholstery

  1. Blot right away. Press a clean, white towel onto the spill to absorb liquid. Do not rub.
  2. Work from the outside in. Blot toward the center to limit spread.
  3. Use cool water sparingly. Lightly dampen the area with cool water, then blot again.
  4. Apply a mild cleaner. If the fabric allows, use a small amount of mild soap solution and blot, then rinse with cool water.
  5. Repeat short cycles. Alternate blotting and light dilution until color transfer slows.
  6. Dry the area. Press dry towels onto the spot to remove moisture from the surface.
  7. Air-dry fully. Let the upholstery dry before deciding if another pass is needed.

What not to do

  • Don’t scrub. Scrubbing can spread pigment and fray fibers.
  • Don’t over-wet the cushion. Excess water can soak the padding and cause rings.
  • Don’t use hot water. Heat can help set dye-based stains.
  • Don’t mix cleaners. Combining products can damage fabrics or create fumes.
  • Don’t speed-dry with heat. Heat can set any remaining pigment.

Notes for common situations

Check the upholstery tag. Follow the fabric code if one is present. If you are unsure, test any cleaner on a hidden area first.

Large or deep spills: If the wine reaches the padding, focus on removing as much liquid as possible without flooding. A stain that reappears after drying may need a second, gentle pass.

Delicate or vintage fabrics: When in doubt, use only blotting and cool water and consult a professional cleaner.
