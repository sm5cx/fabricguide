First Steps for Treating Red Wine Stains on Rugs

Blot up as much red wine as you can right away with clean white towels—pressing down firmly, not rubbing—then dampen the area with a small amount of cool water and keep blotting to dilute and lift the stain. Once the excess is removed, apply a mild cleaning solution in small amounts and continue blotting until the color stops transferring.

For the general approach, see: Red wine on carpet.

Steps to take right away

  1. Blot immediately. Use clean white paper towels or cloths and press straight down. Replace towels as they pick up wine.
  2. Do not rub. If the spill has particles, lift them off gently with a spoon before continuing to blot.
  3. Dilute with cool water. Lightly dampen the stained area (don’t soak it) and blot again to pull more wine out of the fibers.
  4. Mix a gentle cleaner. Combine a few drops of mild dish soap with cool water. Test on an inconspicuous spot first.
  5. Apply and blot. Dab the solution onto the stain with a cloth, working from the outside edge toward the center. Blot, don’t scrub.
  6. Rinse by blotting. Lightly dampen a fresh cloth with plain cool water and blot to remove soap residue.
  7. Dry the area. Press with dry towels until no more moisture transfers. If possible, let it air-dry fully; place a dry towel on top and add light weight to keep it wicking.
  8. Repeat if needed. If color still transfers to the towel, repeat the dilute-and-blot cycle in small passes rather than flooding the rug.

What to avoid

  • Don’t rub or scrub. This can push wine deeper and fuzz or distort the rug’s surface.
  • Don’t use hot water or heat. Heat can set stains and make removal harder.
  • Don’t over-wet the rug. Soaking can drive stain into the backing and slow drying.
  • Don’t use colored towels. Dye can transfer onto the rug when wet.
  • Don’t skip rinsing. Leaving soap behind can attract dirt and cause a sticky spot later.
  • Don’t mix multiple cleaners. Combining products can damage fibers or create residues that are hard to remove.

Notes for your situation

Assess the extent of the stain and soak through: Check whether the wine has reached deeper layers by pressing a dry towel firmly and seeing how much liquid wicks up. If the spill spread widely or soaked through, focus on controlled blotting and thorough drying to prevent lingering discoloration and odor.

Determine the rug's material for appropriate treatment: If you’re not sure what the rug is made of, stick with cool water, a tiny amount of mild dish soap, and gentle blotting. Some materials can be sensitive to stronger chemicals, vigorous agitation, or heavy wetting.

Collect necessary cleaning supplies before starting: Gather white towels, a spoon, a small bowl, cool water, mild dish soap, and optionally a fan for drying. Having everything ready helps you work quickly without letting the stain sit.

Aftercare: Once dry, reassess in good light. If a shadow remains, repeat gentle spot-cleaning rather than escalating to harsh products, and avoid heavy foot traffic until fully dry.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
