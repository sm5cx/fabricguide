Documenting a Stain’s State Before Recovery

Before you try to remove a stain, take a few minutes to document what it is, how it looks, and how it changes over time. Clear notes and photos help you choose a safer treatment and avoid making the stain permanent.

Steps to document a stain before recovery

  1. Pause and avoid further transfer. Stop rubbing, don’t add random products, and keep the stained area from touching other fabric so the “starting condition” stays accurate.
  2. Record the basics. Note the clothing item, fabric type if you know it, and the date/time you noticed the stain. Write down anything relevant you remember about how it happened (spilled, brushed against, unknown source).
  3. Identify the likely stain type. Based on what you know, label it as a best guess (for example: oil/grease, pigment/dye, protein/food, tannin/plant/coffee-like, or unknown). If you’re unsure, mark it as unknown rather than forcing a category.
  4. Do a quick visual assessment. Note the color, shape, and approximate size, plus whether it looks wet, tacky, crusty, or set-in. Check for multiple areas (splatter, ring edges, or a concentrated center).
  5. Check both sides and the surrounding area. Look at the inside and outside of the garment and note whether the stain has bled through, spread, or transferred to seams, cuffs, or lining.
  6. Photograph in good light. Take a few clear photos in natural light or bright indoor light. Capture (1) the whole garment for location context, (2) a mid-range shot, and (3) close-ups that show texture and edges.
  7. Add a size reference and focus on clarity. Include a simple reference (like a coin or ruler) near the stain without covering it. Hold the camera steady and take multiple shots so at least one is sharp and color-accurate.
  8. Repeat after any change. If the stain dries, darkens, spreads, or you do a gentle rinse, take a new photo and note what changed. Keeping a “before/after each attempt” record prevents guesswork later.

What not to do while documenting

  • Don’t rub or scrub “to see what happens.” This can spread the stain or push it deeper into fibers.
  • Don’t apply heat. Avoid hot water, dryers, irons, or hair dryers before you’ve documented and chosen a method, since heat can set many stains.
  • Don’t start mixing products. Combining cleaners before documenting can change the stain’s appearance and make it harder to identify later.
  • Don’t rely on memory. Take photos and notes even if the stain seems obvious; details like edges, texture, and size matter.
  • Don’t use flash as your only photo. Flash can wash out true color; use bright, even light and take multiple angles if needed.

Notes for better stain records

Stain type identification (oil, pigment, protein, etc.)
If the source is known, write it down plainly (e.g., “salad dressing,” “marker,” “egg”). If it’s unknown, describe what you observe (sheen, color intensity, crusting) and keep the label as “unknown” rather than guessing too confidently.

Immediate visual assessment of the stain
Document the stain’s edges (sharp vs. diffuse), any “ring” patterns, and whether it’s raised or flat. These details can help distinguish surface residue from dye-like penetration.

Using photographs for detailed records
Take photos from the same distance and lighting each time if you’re tracking changes. If color accuracy matters, add one photo in natural light and one under indoor light, and keep your size reference consistent.

Practical tip
A simple note format is: item + fabric (if known) + stain source/guess + location on garment + date/time + photos taken. This keeps your documentation quick and repeatable.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
