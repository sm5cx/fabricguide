Is Baking Soda Safe to Use on Red Wine Stains?

Yes—baking soda is generally safe to use on red wine stains on cotton, as long as you test a hidden spot first and avoid harsh scrubbing. It works best as a gentle, absorbent paste after you’ve blotted up as much wine as possible.

How to Use Baking Soda on a Red Wine Stain (Cotton)

  1. Blot immediately. Press a clean cloth or paper towel onto the stain to absorb wine. Don’t rub—rubbing can push pigment deeper into cotton fibers.
  2. Rinse from the back (if you can). Hold the fabric under cool running water with the stain facing down so the water pushes wine out, not through the fabric.
  3. Mix a baking soda paste. Combine baking soda with a small amount of cool water to make a thick, spreadable paste (not runny).
  4. Apply and let it sit. Cover the stained area with the paste and let it sit until it dries or for about 15–30 minutes, whichever comes first.
  5. Lift the paste gently. Scrape off dried paste with a spoon edge or brush it off lightly. Rinse well with cool water.
  6. Repeat if needed. If you still see color, apply fresh paste again. Multiple short rounds are usually safer than one long, aggressive attempt.
  7. Wash normally (check care label). Launder the cotton item as you usually would. Inspect the stain before drying.
  8. Air-dry until the stain is gone. If any trace remains, avoid heat drying and repeat treatment; heat can make leftover dye harder to remove.

What Not to Do

  • Don’t rub or scrub hard. This can spread the stain and roughen cotton fibers, making discoloration more noticeable.
  • Don’t use hot water early on. Start with cool water; heat can set wine pigments.
  • Don’t leave baking soda paste on indefinitely. Let it dry or cap the dwell time, then rinse thoroughly to avoid residue.
  • Don’t mix baking soda with unknown chemicals. If you’ve used other stain products, rinse them out first before trying a new approach.
  • Don’t machine-dry before checking. Dry heat can lock in any remaining stain.

Notes for Your Situation

How long has the stain been there? Fresh stains respond best. If the stain is older or has dried, baking soda may still be safe on cotton but may be less effective; plan on repeating the paste step and rinsing thoroughly between attempts.

What type of fabric is affected? This guidance is for cotton. If the item is a cotton blend, dyed cotton, or has a special finish, do a small spot test first—especially on dark or bright colors—to check for color change or chalky residue.

Have any other treatments been applied previously? If you already tried soap, stain removers, or other home remedies, rinse the area well before applying baking soda so you’re not layering residues. If the stain has been heat-dried or ironed, it may be set; baking soda is still low-risk on cotton, but results may be limited.

Educational guidance only. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.
